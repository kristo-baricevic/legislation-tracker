H.R. 1234 · 119th CongressPassed the House

Student Success Grants Act

What this bill does

This bill would help public colleges keep students in school by funding tutoring, child care, and emergency help. It would also fund research into which approaches help more students graduate.

Who it helps
Students from low-income households and students who are the first in their family to attend college.
The money
Would allow $120 million in federal funding over three years. Congress would still need to approve the actual spending.
How it works
Public colleges apply to the Department of Education, then report each year on how the funding is used and whether students stay enrolled.

What would change

4 changes

More help staying in college

Colleges could use grants to offer tutoring, child care, and emergency support, such as help paying for housing.

Support directed to students facing barriers

The grants would serve students from low-income households and first-generation college students.

Only public colleges could apply. They would need to contribute some of their own funding unless they qualify for a hardship waiver.

A way to find out what works

Independent researchers would compare funded programs and study whether they help students stay enrolled and graduate.

Colleges would have to report results

Each college receiving a grant would report its spending and student enrollment results every year.

A few useful terms

Authorized funding

Money Congress allows a program to receive. A separate spending decision is still needed before that money is available.

First-generation student

For this bill, a student whose parents have not completed a four-year college degree.

Matching funds

Money a college contributes alongside the federal grant. Here, a college generally contributes $1 for every $10 in federal grant money.
