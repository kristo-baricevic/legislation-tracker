## Facts and required answers

### LR-F01 — title, purpose and affected population; section 1; section 2(1)(C)

The Laken Riley Act adds a mandatory-custody category under INA 236(c) involving both specified inadmissibility provisions AND specified crime-related triggers. It is not a rule covering every immigrant arrested for any crime. The title's theft label is narrower than the actual list. Arrest or charge can trigger the category without a conviction; admissions also qualify.

> This Act may be cited as the ``Laken Riley Act''.

> is inadmissible under paragraph (6)(A), (6)(C), or (7) of section 212(a);

> is charged with, is arrested for, is convicted of, admits having committed, or admits committing acts which constitute the essential elements of any burglary, theft, larceny, shoplifting, or assault of a law enforcement officer offense, or any crime that results in death or serious bodily injury to another person,

### LR-F02 — definitions and detainer/custody duties; section 2(2)–(3)

The existing paragraph (2) becomes (4). New paragraph (2) uses the jurisdiction where the acts occurred to define the listed terms, rather than supplying national definitions. New paragraph (3) requires DHS to issue a detainer for covered persons and effectively/expeditiously take custody if not otherwise federally/state/locally detained. It supplies no numerical detention duration or calendar deadline.

> by redesignating paragraph (2) as paragraph (4);

> For purposes of paragraph (1)(E), the terms `burglary', `theft', `larceny', `shoplifting', `assault of a law enforcement officer', and `serious bodily injury' have the meanings given such terms in the jurisdiction in which the acts occurred.

> The Secretary of Homeland Security shall issue a detainer for an alien described in paragraph (1)(E) and, if the alien is not otherwise detained by Federal, State, or local officials, shall effectively and expeditiously take custody of the alien.

### LR-F03 — state enforcement: admission inspection; section 3(a), new INA 235(b)(3)

State AG or another authorized state officer alleging a harmful violation of paragraph (1)/(2) detention/removal requirements receives standing to sue DHS Secretary for appropriate injunctive relief in an appropriate US district court on behalf of the state/residents. The prior paragraph (3) is renumbered (4). See LR-F08/M01 for expedition and harm rule.

> by redesignating paragraph (3) as paragraph (4);

> The attorney general of a State, or other authorized State officer, alleging a violation of the detention and removal requirements under paragraph (1) or (2) that harms such State or its residents shall have standing to bring an action against the Secretary of Homeland Security on behalf of such State or the residents of such State in an appropriate district court of the United States to obtain appropriate injunctive relief.

### LR-F04 — state enforcement: release/bond/parole; section 3(b), INA 236(e), new (f)

Section 236(e) loses “or release” and changes “grant, revocation, or denial” to “revocation or denial”; the full old clause is unresolved. New (f) gives the specified state officials standing for harmful release, bond, or parole actions/decisions under section 236, against AG or DHS Secretary, for injunctions. This language says “any alien,” not just the new theft-related category.

> by striking ``or release'';

> by striking ``grant, revocation, or denial'' and insert ``revocation or denial'';

> The attorney general of a State, or other authorized State officer, alleging an action or decision by the Attorney General or Secretary of Homeland Security under this section to release any alien or grant bond or parole to any alien that harms such State or its residents shall have standing to bring an action against the Attorney General or Secretary of Homeland Security on behalf of such State or the residents of such State in an appropriate district court of the United States to obtain appropriate injunctive relief.

### LR-F05 — state enforcement: visa discontinuance; section 3(c), new INA 243(e)

Specified state officials can sue the Secretary of State for appropriate injunctions over harmful violation of the subsection (d) visa-discontinuance requirement. This is not itself a quoted new blanket visa ban; the referenced subsection (d)'s complete conditions are unresolved.

> The attorney general of a State, or other authorized State officer, alleging a violation of the requirement to discontinue granting visas to citizens, subjects, nationals, and residents as described in subsection (d) that harms such State or its residents shall have standing to bring an action against the Secretary of State on behalf of such State or the residents of such State in an appropriate district court of the United States to obtain appropriate injunctive relief.

### LR-F06 — state enforcement: parole limits and official substitution; section 3(d), INA 212(d)(5)

Replaces “Attorney General” with “Secretary of Homeland Security” throughout the amended paragraph. State officials gain standing to sue DHS Secretary for harmful violation of the referenced limits: parole solely case by case and solely for urgent humanitarian reasons or significant public benefit. Do not turn this enforcement provision into elimination of all parole.

> by striking ``Attorney General'' each place such term appears and inserting ``Secretary of Homeland Security'';

> The attorney general of a State, or other authorized State officer, alleging a violation of the limitation under subparagraph (A) that parole solely be granted on a case-by-case basis and solely for urgent humanitarian reasons or a significant public benefit, that harms such State or its residents shall have standing to bring an action against the Secretary of Homeland Security on behalf of such State or the residents of such State in an appropriate district court of the United States to obtain appropriate injunctive relief.

### LR-F07 — state enforcement: removal-period detention; section 3(e), new INA 241(a)(2)(B)

Labels the existing removal-period detention text subparagraph (A), and permits specified state officials to seek injunctions against DHS Secretary for harmful violations of its detention requirement. The original removal-period rules are not reproduced in full.

> by striking ``During the removal period,'' and inserting the following:

> The attorney general of a State, or other authorized State officer, alleging a violation of the detention requirement under subparagraph (A) that harms such State or its residents shall have standing to bring an action against the Secretary of Homeland Security on behalf of such State or the residents of such State in an appropriate district court of the United States to obtain appropriate injunctive relief.

### LR-F08 — expedited cases; section 3(a)–(e)

All five inserted state-enforcement provisions require docket advancement and expedited disposition to the greatest extent practicable, not a fixed number of days. Section 3(a) says “paragraph,” 3(b)/(c) say “subsection,” and 3(d)/(e) say “subparagraph.”

Section 3(a):

> The court shall advance on the docket and expedite the disposition of a civil action filed under this paragraph to the greatest extent practicable.

Sections 3(b), 3(c):

> The court shall advance on the docket and expedite the disposition of a civil action filed under this subsection to the greatest extent practicable.

Sections 3(d), 3(e):

> The court shall advance on the docket and expedite the disposition of a civil action filed under this subparagraph to the greatest extent practicable.

### LR-F09 — limited injunction-bar exception; section 3(f), new INA 242(f)(3)

Paragraph (1)'s limitation does not apply to actions under exactly the cited provisions: 235(b)(3), 236(e)/(f), and 241(a)(2)(B). Do not claim every action newly permitted by section 3 is listed: 243(e) and 212(d)(5)(C) are not named here. Full meaning of the old paragraph (1) remains externally unresolved.

> Paragraph (1) shall not apply to an action brought pursuant to section 235(b)(3), subsections (e) or (f) of section 236, or section 241(a)(2)(B).

## Every explicit financial provision

These are standing/harm thresholds, not appropriations, fines, grants, detention budgets, compensation payments, or damages awards. Each uses **in excess of $100**, not $100 or more. Nonfinancial harm is not excluded: each says harm “including” financial harm. No measurement period, aggregation method, or upper limit is specified; do not invent one. Purpose/restrictions are tied separately to each enforcement route below.

### LR-M01 — admission-inspection enforcement harm; section 3(a), new INA 235(b)(3)

Purpose: harm qualification for LR-F03. Amount: financial harm >$100 included; other harm not excluded. Timeframe: unspecified. Restriction: state or its residents harmed, in the specified enforcement action; no money is awarded by this clause.

> For purposes of this paragraph, a State or its residents shall be considered to have been harmed if the State or its residents experience harm, including financial harm in excess of $100.

### LR-M02 — release/bond/parole enforcement harm; section 3(b), new INA 236(f)

Purpose: harm qualification for LR-F04. Amount: >$100 financial harm included, not exclusive. Timeframe: unspecified. Restriction: state/resident harm in this release/bond/parole action; no payment.

> For purposes of this subsection, a State or its residents shall be considered to have been harmed if the State or its residents experience harm, including financial harm in excess of $100.

### LR-M03 — visa-discontinuance enforcement harm; section 3(c), new INA 243(e)

Purpose: harm qualification for LR-F05. Amount: >$100 financial harm included, not exclusive. Timeframe: unspecified. Restriction: state/resident harm in the visa-discontinuance action; no payment. This identical quotation has a distinct section 3(c) source occurrence.

> For purposes of this subsection, a State or its residents shall be considered to have been harmed if the State or its residents experience harm, including financial harm in excess of $100.

### LR-M04 — case-by-case parole enforcement harm; section 3(d), new INA 212(d)(5)(C)

Purpose: harm qualification for LR-F06. Amount: >$100 financial harm included, not exclusive. Timeframe: unspecified. Restriction: state/resident harm in the specified parole-limits action; no payment.

> For purposes of this subparagraph, a State or its residents shall be considered to have been harmed if the State or its residents experience harm, including financial harm in excess of $100.

### LR-M05 — removal-period detention enforcement harm; section 3(e), new INA 241(a)(2)(B)

Purpose: harm qualification for LR-F07. Amount: >$100 financial harm included, not exclusive. Timeframe: unspecified. Restriction: state/resident harm in removal-period detention action; no payment. This identical quotation has a distinct section 3(e) source occurrence.
